1. Problem fit and real-world impact Weight 25%

Question 1.1Is the problem clearly defined?

Yes. The problem is not “AI art” or “copyright on the internet.” It is a pre-publication decision that organisations already have to make, and that they currently make with the wrong unit of analysis.

Before a brand, agency, marketplace or publisher presses publish, someone must answer three different questions about the same file:

  • Reuse. Does this image contain a work that is already registered in the sources we are allowed to search?
  • Origin. What evidence exists that a generative model was involved in producing it?
  • Resemblance. Does it resemble a specific living creator’s body of work, even if no copy of a stored file is present?

The market sells one number for all three — “87% AI”, “93% similar”, “match found.” Each collapse causes a different harm: origin-as-guilt, similarity-as-infringement, style-as-authorship. A fourth failure is quieter: when a tool searches an incomplete database and returns “no matches,” users hear “this image is clean.” That is a definition of the problem, not a slogan: publishers need a structured record of what was checked, what was found, and what was decided — not a verdict dressed as a percentage.

Question 1.2Is the problem meaningful for the intended users?

Yes, for people who already carry the cost. A single disputed campaign image can exceed a year of software spend. Marketplace reviewers process takedowns with no shared evidence packet and no consistent standard between shifts. Independent illustrators have no practical way to register a work so that the organisations most likely to reuse it actually consult that registration.

Intended userWhy it is meaningful to them
Creative agency (beachhead) Client contracts now demand originality warranties and AI disclosure. The agency cannot currently attach a defensible check to the invoice.
Brand in-house studio Dozens of agencies and freelancers feed the same brand. There is no consistent pre-publication standard and no audit trail six months later.
Marketplace / UGC platform Human review queues do not scale. Appeals need structured evidence, not a moderator’s memory.
Publisher / newsroom Synthetic-media disclosure is moving from etiquette into policy. An editor’s gut is not a record.
Independent creator Not the paying customer. They need a timestamped registration that is actually searched when a brand checks an asset — funded by the checking side.

A Bangladeshi studio delivering to US/UK/EU clients is a concrete local instance of the same problem: they can lose a Western contract because they cannot show they checked, not because they stole anything.

Question 1.3Does the solution address a genuine real-world need?

Yes. The need already exists as unpaid labour and residual legal risk. People reverse-image search, squint, and hope. That workflow fails on the cases that matter: a mirrored crop inside a social screenshot; a file with no Content Credentials; a style-mimic that copies no stored pixels.

CreatorProof addresses the need that is actually in the job description — produce a check you can stand behind — by:

  • searching a declared catalog and stating coverage before any “no match” language is allowed;
  • keeping copy, origin and profile as separate evidence, never averaged into an infringement score;
  • turning findings into the customer’s own PASS / REVIEW / BLOCK policy, with reason codes;
  • sealing the packet so a counterparty can verify it without trusting our database.

It does not pretend to replace a lawyer, crawl the open internet, or certify that a work is human-made. Those are not the customer’s operational need; a dated, scoped, inspectable record is.

Yes — in hours displaced, incidents avoided, and disputes that have a file instead of a Slack thread. We model the beachhead (a 20–200 person agency, ~500 assets/month) as:

Impact after adoption is “fewer blind publishes and a packet you can hand a client.” Impact we will not claim until a customer corpus exists: a published false-positive rate on that corpus.

2. Innovation and idea quality Weight 20%

Question 2.1Is the idea creative?

The creative move is not a new neural net. It is refusing the product category the market is in — AI that issues a verdict — and building the category organisations actually need: AI that produces evidence, with a proof that the evidence existed.

Four design choices are unusual in combination:

  • Lane separation enforced in code. Copy, origin and profile have different data, failure modes and legal meanings. They are never fused into an “infringement percentage.”
  • Coverage before verdict. NO_MATCH_IN_CHECKED_SOURCES is impossible unless declared catalog coverage is COMPLETE. Incomplete searches cannot impersonate clearance.
  • AI nominates; geometry verifies. A learned descriptor may propose a candidate. A match requires independent physical alignment and aligned-pixel corroboration.
  • Joint witnessing. A second organisation can co-attest the same evidence commitment on chain, so a decision is not a single vendor’s word.

The product’s most distinctive behaviour is abstention: it will not emit its strongest origin claim until independently verified, calibrated evidence families agree — and today it says they do not. Shipping a system that visibly refuses to overclaim is the idea.

Question 2.2Does the solution provide a meaningful improvement over existing approaches?

Existing tools optimise for one of the three questions and imply the other two.

Approach todayWhat it actually answersWhat users wrongly hear
Reverse image search Near-duplicate pages the crawler has seen “Not infringing” if nothing famous appears
AI-image detectors A score under a specific training distribution “This is / is not AI,” as a fact
Style / “who painted this” apps Nearest embedding in a scraped set Authorship, and therefore copying
Manual legal review A real opinion, slowly, expensively Unavailable at upload time

CreatorProof’s improvement is structural: scoped search, separated questions, policy the customer owns, and a packet a third party can hash. A PASS means “nothing against your policy in the sources we finished searching,” which is a weaker sentence and a more useful one. We also recover transformations reverse-image search often misses — mirror, crop, re-encode, screenshot chrome — because the copy lane is built for reposts, not for web-index hits.

What we do not claim as an improvement: we do not search the open internet. A tool that claims to have checked everywhere produces a “no match” that means nothing. Declared catalogs are the honest scope.

Question 2.3Is AI being used purposefully rather than simply added to the project?

Yes. Every question the product answers is a model question. The architecture exists to constrain what those models are allowed to conclude. That is the opposite of a chatbot bolted onto a dashboard.

Where AI is load-bearing:

  • SSCD retrieves likely copies from the catalog (including regional crops so a collage still nominates).
  • Community Forensics (and optional Sightengine) supply origin signal; C2PA is checked first because a signed manifest beats any detector.
  • CSD embeddings support creator-profile resemblance, with a content control so “same subject” is not mistaken for “same hand.”
  • Tesseract reads visible generator watermarks as a forgeable review cue, never as provenance.

Where AI is deliberately not allowed to finish the job:

  • A neural score cannot declare a copy. SIFT/ORB + USAC/MAGSAC homography and aligned structure must agree.
  • Origin scores are labelled ORIGINAL_PRESERVING_QUALITY_WEIGHTED_ENSEMBLE_NOT_PROBABILITY. They are not percentages of truth.
  • The profile lane cannot escalate policy on its own. Resemblance is not copying.
  • Optional OpenRouter text only explains structured metrics. It cannot change retrieval, match status, or policy.

Purpose is also governance: SHA-256-pinned model bundles, an evidence-family registry so two clones of one detector cannot fake independence, a qualification ladder that self-reports RUNTIME_READY rather than “production,” and automatic_promotion_allowed: false. AI is used to see; rules are used to decide what seeing is allowed to mean.

3. Technical implementation Weight 20%

Question 3.1Has the team developed a functional prototype or convincing technical concept?

A functional prototype, not a concept deck. CreatorProof v0.10.0 is a running system:

  • API. FastAPI 0.10.0 — catalog, scans, policy, review, proof, network co-attestation, webhooks, usage meters.
  • Web. Next.js 16.3 / React 19 — marketing landing, Artist portal, User (checker) portal, Evidence Microscope, proof panel.
  • Models on the copy/origin/profile path load from a pinned bundle (SSCD, Community Forensics, optional CSD). None of those three is a UI placeholder.
  • Proof. Canonical SHA-256 packet, Ed25519 / COSE Sign1, RFC 6962-style Merkle log, EAS attestation pipeline on Base Sepolia when configured.

Public interface: creatorproof.uaenorth.cloudapp.azure.com. Source: github.com/AhnafZ778/CreatorProof. End-to-end scans execute against the FastAPI backend on the same host. The Azure app is the real product UI; detection is not simulated in the browser.

Question 3.2Does the AI functionality work as described?

Yes, within the scope we state. We measured the copy lane on a harness we wrote (scripts/copy_detection_bench.py): 14 source images × 26 real-world transformations = 364 positive cases, plus 115 unrelated-image negatives.

98.08%positives returned MATCH_FOUND
99.45%flagged match or review
0false matches on 115 negatives
14/14horizontal mirrors recovered after a dedicated retry path

Residual failures are named: two misses, both a flat repetitive graphic under aggressive crop or small rotation — SIFT cannot lock distinctive keypoints. We did not loosen thresholds to erase those misses, because a false match is worse than a review referral.

Origin works as described, including the uncomfortable part: missing C2PA is NOT_PRESENT (unknown, not human); the strongest origin class is suppressed until calibrated independent families exist; we currently have one verified family, so the system abstains from that class. Profile resemblance ranks with CSLS density correction and stays advisory.

These numbers are our pipeline on our transformation set, not a public leaderboard. That is disclosed in the product, not only here.

Question 3.3Is the technical approach logical and feasible?

Logical because each stage uses the tool that is good at that stage, and feasible because every stage already runs.

01 · Retrieve Learned SSCD (and pHash fallback) nominates catalog works, including regional crops for collages.
02 · Verify Classical SIFT/ORB, ratio test, USAC/MAGSAC homography, mirror retry, aligned luminance/gradient/SSIM consensus.
03 · Decide Rules Typed fusion + versioned customer policy. Fusion is not a second neural net, because a customer must be able to justify it.
04 · Prove Crypto Deterministic packet hash, issuer signature, Merkle inclusion, optional 32-byte EAS attest. Artwork never goes on chain.

Feasibility constraints are designed in: image size caps; catalog completeness bounds (above the reference limit, a scan cannot claim complete no-match); crash-safe persist-before-broadcast for chain writes; idempotency bound to bytes + catalog + intended use. The approach scales first as an API with remote embeddings (the Render profile), then as GPU workers — not as a laptop demo that cannot leave the laptop.

Question 3.4Can the team clearly explain how the system works?

Yes. One sentence, then the moving parts.

The system in one sentence

You declare a catalog and an intended use, we run three independent evidence lanes over the image, we apply your policy, and we hand you a signed packet whose hash can be checked without trusting us.

A creator registers a reference in the Artist portal; an AI-origin gate can refuse enrollment and returns the score and threshold so the refusal is contestable. A checker drops a candidate in the User portal, sees catalog size before the scan (so an empty catalog cannot look like clearance), watches stage progress, then reads: one bottom line, three lane cards, an Evidence Microscope with actual keypoint correspondences, and a proof status. Policy is a versioned document with a dry-run endpoint. Proof is four independently checkable layers: canonical hash, signature, transparency log, public anchor.

This PDF, the README, and the live UI use the same vocabulary on purpose: match status, policy action, coverage, not “infringement %.”

02

Problem

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 1. Also the video-pitch problem beat.

What real-world problem are you solving?

Commercial image pipelines move faster than they can account for. A file can be generated, commissioned, scraped, mirrored, screenshot and published under a brand name in an afternoon. The organisation still has to decide whether publishing it is defensible, and today that decision is unstructured: a person, a reverse-image tab, and no artefact that survives contact with a client, a moderator appeal, or a lawyer six months later.

Generative models made origin visually ambiguous. Style mimicry became a one-prompt operation. Content Credentials (C2PA) shipped in cameras and editors, which created a standard to build on — and a new failure mode when the manifest is absent. Disclosure obligations moved into contracts and platform policy. The problem is the missing record at the moment of publication.

Who experiences this problem?

Production and legal-ops people inside agencies and brands; trust-and-safety reviewers on marketplaces; picture desks in newsrooms; and the illustrators whose work is the catalog those organisations ought to be searching. Neither the brand manager nor the artist is the villain. The villain is a supply chain with no receipts.

Why does it matter?

Because the cost of a wrong publish is asymmetric. A false accusation damages a living artist. A missed reuse damages a rights-holder and the publisher’s client relationship. A confident “no match” on an incomplete search manufactures liability. Creative work is a large commercial surface with none of the batch records that every other regulated industry takes for granted. It matters now because the old heuristic — “I can tell by looking” — stopped working.

03

Solution

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 2. Also the video-pitch solution beat.

What did you build?

CreatorProof is a pre-publication creative-rights evidence API and two portals. It runs three independent AI lanes over a candidate image, keeps their findings separate, applies a versioned customer policy to produce PASS, REVIEW or BLOCK, and seals the result into a signed evidence packet. Optionally, a 32-byte commitment is attested on a public chain. The artwork never is.

How does the solution address the problem?

Stored-work copy

Learned retrieval nominates; geometry and aligned pixels verify. Answers which registered work is present, and where in the frame.

AI origin

C2PA first, then a detector ensemble under stress views, then OCR of visible labels. Answers what evidence of generative involvement exists — and how strong it is.

Creator profile

Style embeddings against consent-backed profiles, with a content control. Advisory only. Answers resemblance, never authorship.

Coverage is reported before any clearance language. Policy belongs to the customer, so two tenants can treat the same findings differently and both have an audit trail. Proof makes the trail portable: a counterparty hashes the packet, checks the signature, and — if anchored — checks a chain nobody in the deal controls.

AI implementation

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 3: models, APIs, agents, data, automation, workflows, limitations.

Models

Model Architecture Runs Role
SSCD disc_mixup ResNet-50 family, 512-d Local · ~95 MB Copy-detection retrieval. Meta AI Research; trained for copies, not generic similarity.
CSD ViT-L ViT-L, 768-d style Local · ~2.3 GB Creator-profile resemblance. Optional; transparent low-level style fallback if absent.

Tesseract OCR 5.3.4 LSTM text Local CLI Visible “AI generated” labels. Review signal; never trusted provenance.

Neural artefacts are pinned by SHA-256 in the model-bundle manifest and validated at startup. A silently swapped weight file is a boot failure, not a mystery later.

APIs and agents

There is no autonomous agent that publishes, sues, or “decides infringement.” The product is a request/response evidence service: POST /v1/scans returns a scan id; detection runs asynchronously; the UI polls stages. Optional OpenRouter is an explainer over structured metrics only. Optional remote embedding HTTP services supply SSCD/CSD vectors in the lean Render deployment so the API container does not load PyTorch.

Data

Tenant catalogs of registered reference works (fingerprints, cached descriptors, claim state). Consent-backed creator-profile manifests. Candidate bytes retained on a short window. PostgreSQL with RLS for tenant isolation; SQLite for local development; private object storage for media. Evaluation data for the copy benchmark is 14 sources × 26 transforms, produced by us, not a scraped public leaderboard. No personal data is written on chain — only a 32-byte hash.

Automation and AI workflows

  1. Intake: image + catalog + intended use + idempotency key.
  2. Copy workflow: regional SSCD nomination → geometric verify (mirror retry if needed) → aligned structure → rule-based fusion.
  3. Origin workflow: C2PA → optional Sightengine on original bytes → local detector under ten stress views → OCR → family-aware fusion with abstention.
  4. Profile workflow: CSD (or transparent fallback) → CSLS ranking → content confound control → advisory card only.
  5. Policy workflow: versioned rules consume typed findings; dry-run available; default informational origin cannot independently flip policy.
  6. Proof workflow: canonicalise → sign → Merkle append → optional EAS attest / counterparty co-attest.

Limitations (AI)

  • There is no universal AI-image detector. We refuse to label fused scores as probabilities.
  • High-authority origin outcomes require calibrated independent evidence families. Calibration on a customer domain is not present; the bundle is RUNTIME_READY.
  • Copy accuracy is measured on our transformation harness, not on a publisher’s archive.
  • Style resemblance is not attribution and cannot escalate policy alone.
  • Catalog search is declared-scope only. We do not crawl the web.
  • AI coding assistants (Cursor and frontier models) were used to implement and test. Thresholds, scope boundaries and honesty constraints were human decisions.

How the prototype works

The browser never holds an API key. Next.js route handlers talk to FastAPI. A scan is accepted immediately, processed in the background, and recovered by id. Reusing an idempotency key with different bytes, catalog or intended use returns 409 IDEMPOTENCY_PAYLOAD_MISMATCH rather than a silent stale result. Reviewers see coverage, a bottom line, three lanes, then forensic overlays only when they ask. Proof status distinguishes a local Merkle receipt from a mined EAS anchor and will not conflate them in the UI.

User experience

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 5. How someone actually uses the product.

Creator — Artist portal /artist

  1. Opens the Artist portal. Sees library count, whether a creator profile has enough works (≥3), and asserted claims waiting for corroboration.
  2. Drops in a reference image, names the work, states a claimant and catalog.
  3. The origin lane runs as an admission gate. If the fused score exceeds the configured threshold, registration is refused and the refusal shows score, threshold and summary — contestable, not a silent 403.
  4. On success they hold a registration receipt. The work is now searchable by checkers using that catalog.

Publisher / reviewer — User portal /user

  1. Sees how many works the named catalog currently holds before scanning, so an empty catalog cannot look like a clean bill of health.
  2. Drops a candidate (or uses a prepared demo scenario). Stages stream: intake, retrieval, geometry, origin, policy, proof.
  3. Reads the bottom line first (policy action + match status), then the three independent question cards.
  4. Opens the Evidence Microscope only if they need to disagree with the machine on the same pixels the machine used: inliers, transfer error, aligned region. Connection lines stay quiet until hover unless the reviewer asks for all of them.
  5. Opens proof: packet hash, signature, log inclusion, anchor status. Optionally a counterparty co-attests.

The experience is built for a producer under time pressure, not for a researcher. Forensic density is progressive disclosure. The product copy on screen matches the legal boundary in this PDF: evidence and policy, not infringement.

07

Responsible AI

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 6: privacy, security, bias, transparency, safety, human oversight.

Privacy

Catalog images stay in tenant storage. Chain writes are a hash, never the artwork, never a name. Creator profiles are consent-backed manifests, not a scrape of the internet.

Security

API keys map to tenant + role. RLS denies unbound queries. Browser holds no secrets. Idempotency and crash-safe chain persistence prevent duplicate or lost writes. Credentials are not in the repository.

Bias and error posture

False matches are treated as more damaging than misses. Profile hubness is corrected with CSLS. Tiny catalogs cannot manufacture high-confidence creator attribution. Multiplicity correction applies when many profiles are compared.

Transparency

Every result carries coverage, reason codes, model-bundle identity, and limitations. Scores are not labelled probabilities. Local Merkle is not labelled as blockchain. Origin absence is unknown.

Safety

The system cannot authorise a rights-based pass on uncorroborated claims. Informational origin cannot independently change policy in the default mode. Incomplete scope routes to REVIEW, never to silent PASS.

Human oversight

REVIEW is a first-class outcome. The microscope exists so a human can overrule with shared evidence. Model promotion requires a human record; automatic promotion is disabled. Co-attestation is a human organisation binding its key.

Impact and future potential

Official Rules — Submission PDF item 8. Also video-pitch impact and future beats.

Impact

Near-term impact is operational: fewer blind publishes, fewer hours of unstructured reverse image search, and a packet a studio in Dhaka can attach to a delivery for a London client. Creator-side impact is that registration is free and is actually consulted by the organisations who pay — the same incentive design that made content-ID systems work, without claiming we are YouTube.

Wider impact, if the packet becomes a habit: commercial image supply chains gain an audit primitive they currently lack, and “no match” stops meaning “we didn’t look.” That is real-world impact under the ideathon theme: measurable, situated in existing jobs, and honest about remaining legal work that still belongs to humans.

Future potential

  • Product. Agency wedge → brand pull-through via the packet → marketplace and stock-platform partnerships.
  • Network. Shared catalogs and co-attestation turn a vendor into a standard. Leaving means leaving jointly witnessed history.
  • Geography. Bangladesh as a production hub serving disclosure-heavy Western buyers is a launch market, not a limit.
  • Category. Evidence infrastructure for creative publishing, analogous to batch records in other industries — not another detector widget.
